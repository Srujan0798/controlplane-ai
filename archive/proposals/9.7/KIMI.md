## 1. Overall Diagnosis

**Current true strength: 9.3/10.**  
The architecture is coherent, the narrative spine is sharp, and the deck/video are disciplined. The system already wins the room on differentiation. What prevents 10/10 is not ambition or depth — it is **three specific self-inflicted wounds** that a serious judge will spot: fake numbers posing as credibility, one contradictory close line, and one unverifiable optimism.

**Single biggest remaining weakness:**  
Slide 3’s FNR report block shows `94.2%` and `5.8% ± 1.1`. Even with the “illustrative format” label, specific percentages in a concept deck read as fabricated claims. This turns the strongest credibility move in the entire system — *we publish what we miss* — into a potential credibility collapse. One judge muttering “they made up numbers” undermines every other hard-won trust signal.

**Single strongest asset that must be protected at all costs:**  
The **entitlement check** (Responsibility axis) combined with **inverted burden of proof**. It is the only mechanism in the set that is deterministic, sub-millisecond, catches a real enterprise incident (retrieval-side auth failure), and is structurally impossible for any output-only competitor to replicate. If anything crowds this out or softens it, the differentiation dies.

## 2. Ranked Critical Weaknesses

### 1. Slide 3 FNR block shows fabricated percentages
**Diagnosis:** `94.2%`, `5.8%`, and `41 ms` are specific values in a Round 1 concept deck. The “illustrative format” disclaimer is small and subordinate; the eye hits the numbers first.  
**Why it matters:** The entire Slide 3 argument is “we are credible because we admit fallibility.” Fabricated precision destroys the frame from inside. It is the most dangerous line in all four documents.  
**Fix:** Replace every variable number with `__._%` or `--`. Keep only `100% (deterministic)` for entitlement violations, since that is architecturally true by design, not measured. Make the block unmistakably a template.

### 2. Stage 3 narrative spine closes with “when it can’t, the money doesn’t move”
**Diagnosis:** This line implies **Block**, but the frozen matrix routes R3 × unsupported-categorical to **Escalate** (held + evidence packet). Stage 5 correctly fixes this; Stage 3 still carries the contradiction.  
**Why it matters:** A judge who reads the Stage 3 document (still source of truth) and cross-references the matrix will catch the mismatch. Precision is the brand; imprecision is the crack.  
**Fix:** Kill the line in Stage 3. Promote Stage 5’s close as the sole source of truth: *“Now nothing acts until it can prove it should.”*

### 3. Stage 5 Beat 4: “A human resolves it in seconds”
**Diagnosis:** Unverifiable time promise in a systems-clean narrative.  
**Why it matters:** “In seconds” is marketing register. It is the only soft claim in an otherwise hardened script.  
**Fix:** Replace with *“A human receives the claim, the candidate spans, and the verdict.”* Remove the time promise entirely.

### 4. Cost axis lacks visceral stakes equivalent to Performance and Responsibility
**Diagnosis:** Performance has ₹1,84,000 moving. Responsibility has unauthorized data access. Cost has “four of nine steps grounded nothing” — conceptually pure, emotionally flat.  
**Why it matters:** In a 3-minute video, the third read must land with equal weight or it feels like an appendix. The Cost axis is currently the “and also” beat.  
**Fix:** Add one consequence clause to the Cost read: *“Four of nine steps grounded nothing. The meter ran on all nine.”* This gives the axis a sting without changing the mechanism.

### 5. Slide 1 visual density risks 8-second absorption failure
**Diagnosis:** Six claim nodes, three leader lines, a boundary, a gate glyph, span tags, and supporting text.  
**Why it matters:** If a judge cannot parse the graph in <8 seconds, the reframe fails before the argument begins.  
**Fix:** Reduce to **3 claims** (2 bound, 1 unbound) on the slide. Keep 6 in the video where animation carries the density. The slide only needs to prove the structure, not the ratio.

## 5. Deck + Video Consistency & Visual Discipline

**Mismatch 1: Fake numbers**  
Stage 4 Slide 3 shows `94.2%` / `5.8%` / `41 ms`. Stage 5 Beat 4 says “we publish our own miss rate” without numbers. **The deck is more specific than the video, which is backwards.** The video should be the looser medium, not the deck. Fix: Blank the numbers in the deck.

**Mismatch 2: Claim count**  
Stage 4 Slide 1 shows 6 claims. Stage 5 Beat 3 describes 6 claims. Consistent, but the slide may fail absorption. Fix: 3 claims on the slide, 6 in the video.

**Visual risk: Slide 2 bottom strip**  
The actuator chips (`BLOCK` · `EDIT` · `ESCALATE` · `PASS + ANNOTATE`) plus the latency line are at risk of feeling crowded. **They are correctly subordinate,** but if font hierarchy is not ruthlessly enforced, the strip competes with the matrix. Ensure the matrix occupies ≥60% of vertical space and the strip is no more than 12%.

**Visual risk: Slide 3 left column**  
Four struck-through claims with corrections beneath. In 8 seconds, a judge reads top-to-bottom. If the struck-through text is too prominent, they spend 4 seconds reading claims we *reject*. Fix: Struck-through text at 40% opacity, corrections at full weight. The eye should land on the correction, not the rejection.
