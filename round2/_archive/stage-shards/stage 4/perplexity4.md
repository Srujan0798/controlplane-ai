Existing approaches fail at the exact point that matters:

- **LLM-as-judge wrappers** form an opinion about output after generation; they do not prove what the model was actually given and do not carry the caller’s identity into verification.
- **Static guardrails** inspect surface form. They do not catch a fabricated clause, a valid-looking wrong amount, or an HR fact retrieved for the wrong employee.
- **RAG groundedness checkers** typically inspect retrieval alone, average across a response, and do not distinguish a draft from a payment.
- **Observability tools** explain failure after delivery. They do not prevent an action from committing.
- **Confidence thresholds** fail by definition against confidently wrong output.

## 2. Solution Design Summary

ControlPlane.ai is an admission-control layer for AI systems. At context assembly, it records the evidence actually supplied to the model as provenance-bearing `SPAN`s: `source_id · ACL · content_hash · offsets`; it then builds one `STEP → SPAN → CLAIM → ACTION` Evidence Ledger as generation and tool use proceed. ## 4. Business Case & Impact Logic

ControlPlane creates value by changing what an enterprise can safely permit an AI system to do. It does not sell a generic promise to “make AI trustworthy”; it makes the proof requirement for each action explicit and inspectable.

| Value mechanism | How value is created | What the buyer can verify |
|---|---|---|
| Avoided wrong actions | Unsupported claims cannot authorize high-blast-radius actions; R3 actions are held or escalated rather than released on plausible text | Action logs show attempted commit, matrix cell, evidence gap, and `committed=false` |
| Reduced unauthorized disclosure | A claim grounded in an ACL-excluded span is caught even when semantically correct | Principal, source ACL, binding edge, and entitlement decision are retained in the ledger |
| Lower review burden | The matrix avoids treating every uncertainty as a hard stop; R0/R1 can pass or annotate while R2/R3 demand stronger control | Actuator distribution, override rate, and matrix-cell frequency by route |
| Fewer opaque escalations | Escalations include the failed claim, candidate evidence, verdict, and diff | Reviewers receive an evidence packet rather than a generic alert |
| Reduced dead compute | The graph can be walked backward from accepted claims to identify steps that grounded nothing | Exact unused retrieval/tool steps, not estimated token waste |
| Faster audit and incident reconstruction | Each decision retains source hash, ACL, principal, policy version, verifier version, action, and latency | A reviewer can replay `action → claim → span → source → entitlement` |
| Safer autonomy expansion | Routes begin in shadow; enforcement is earned with route-specific evidence rather than enabled globally | Shadow counterfactuals, audited interventions, override rates, and route readiness records |

The economic logic is direct:

1. AI agents become useful when they can act, not only draft.
2. Acting systems create asymmetric downside: one wrong payment, disclosure, or publication can outweigh many correct answers.
3. A generic output score cannot justify an action because it does not identify the evidence, the caller entitlement, or the consequence.
4. ControlPlane reduces this downside by requiring an action-relevant proof path and preserving the record needed to challenge the decision later.
5. As route evidence accumulates, the enterprise can move a route from shadow to constrained enforcement to earned autonomy.

No ROI percentage should be claimed before deployment data exists. The defensible business case is an operating model: measure prevented action attempts, entitlement violations, reviewer overrides, dead compute, latency, and the plane’s own per-route false-negative rate. ## 6. Key Risks & Mitigations

| Risk class | Risk | Exact mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | A synthesized or multi-hop claim appears supported because a shallow span looks similar | Derived and aggregative claims bypass ordinary entailment: recompute from spans or return `UNKNOWN`; `UNKNOWN` never becomes `SUPPORTED`; high-R unknowns route through the matrix |
| Technical | A source document is poisoned, stale, or factually wrong | Preserve immutable source ID and content hash for every binding; track source-level failure concentration; quarantine or de-rank sources through versioned policy. ControlPlane defends the claim-to-evidence link, not the truth of the source |
| Technical | Upstream IAM/ACL metadata is missing or wrong | Enforce carried ACLs, log ACL gaps, treat missing access metadata conservatively on privileged routes, and create source-owner remediation signals. Do not claim to repair IAM |
| Operational | Over-flagging causes teams to bypass the system | Use blast-radius pricing, check-worthiness filtering, surgical edits, shadow-first rollout, route-specific thresholds, override reason codes, and automatic rollback if human override rate exceeds 3× baseline |
| Operational | Under-flagging creates liability | `UNSUPPORTED` default, deterministic entitlement checks, action gate at commit boundary, R2/R3 fail closed or escalate, audited pass sampling, and per-route FNR reporting |
| Operational | Latency or verifier timeout creates a bypass path | Provenance is indexed at context assembly; Lane 1 is deterministic and inline; timeout yields `UNKNOWN`; R0/R1 fail open with annotation while R2/R3 fail closed or escalate |
| Adoption | Teams resist integration work or fear disruption | Require only the truthful integration point: a context-assembly hook, OpenAI-compatible proxy, and action adapter. Begin in shadow; prove value before enforcement |
| Adoption | Buyers mistake the product for a generic guardrail | Demo and sales motion begin at a held transaction and trace backward to missing evidence, not at a dashboard or abstract safety claim |
| Governance | Policy changes quietly weaken control or create inconsistent behavior | Versioned deterministic policy DAG; static invariant validation; shadow replay; canary; named approval; append-only audit trail; rollback on override/error-budget breach |
| Regulatory | Rules differ by geography and evolve | Additive regional/industry overlays can tighten data handling, retention, escalation, and action allow-lists, but cannot weaken provenance, entitlement, or the frozen matrix |

The system does not claim to eliminate hallucinations, privacy risk, bias, or operational error. Its claim is narrower and testable: claims without sufficient proof or entitlement cannot be allowed to authorize actions beyond the route’s admitted control boundary. ## 8. Fidelity Self-Check

- **Default = `UNSUPPORTED`:** Preserved. The proposal never frames missing proof as low confidence, benign uncertainty, or implicit permission.
- **Entitlement:** Preserved. Caller-versus-source ACL set-membership remains deterministic, always on, and independent of semantic plausibility.
- **Exact R×S matrix:** Preserved. The business model uses route configuration and blast-radius inputs, not route-specific matrix cells or invented verdict tiers.
- **Hard gate on actions:** Preserved. Text can stream behind hold-back; commit-capable actions remain controlled by the Action Interlock.
- **No elimination claim:** Preserved. ControlPlane does not claim to eliminate hallucination, bias, privacy risk, or source corruption.
- **No LLM-as-judge or confidence primary mechanism:** Preserved. Model-assisted extraction or bounded entailment may produce typed evidence inputs; the Action Interlock remains a pure rule engine. No confidence or composite risk score determines disposition.
- **Bias:** Preserved as asynchronous, route-level counterfactual measurement with a confidence interval. It is not treated as a live per-response verdict or matrix column.
